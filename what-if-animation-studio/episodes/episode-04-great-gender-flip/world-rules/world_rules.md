# WHAT IF...? E04 — The Great Gender Flip: World Rules

These rules keep every scene consistent. If a shot breaks a rule, the shot is wrong, not the rule.

## The event
- At 00:00:00 (local midnight, everywhere at once) a violet–cyan shockwave passes over the world.
- It is magical and fictional. It is not a scientific prediction and not a depiction of real-world gender transition.
- The change is instant, painless and fully clothed. No nudity, no anatomy, no body-horror. On screen it is a sparkle + morph of face, hair and silhouette only.

## Two simultaneous changes
1. **Transformation A — bodies.** In this deliberately simplified binary premise, people who had male physical characteristics now have female ones, and vice versa.
2. **Transformation B — social expectations.** Conventional gendered expectations (who pays, who cooks, who "leads", who may cry, what to wear) swap over.

## Continuity rules (enforced in every scene)
| # | Rule | How it shows on screen |
|---|------|------------------------|
| 1 | Everyone keeps memories, personality, knowledge and sense of self. | Alex is still dramatic; Maya is still calm and witty. Same jacket colours, eyebrows, hair signatures. |
| 2 | Public norms, ads, etiquette and social expectations follow the swapped conventions immediately. | Billboards, poster, app prompts, notifications all show crossed-out old words replaced by swapped ones. |
| 3 | Institutions update, but some technology and paperwork stay outdated. | The manager's ID badge still shows the old photo. The scientist's paper is "v1.0". |
| 4 | Characters are confused because memory conflicts with surroundings. | Reaction shots: double-takes, "?" icons, shrugs. |
| 5 | No one gains new skills automatically. | Alex still can't cook any better; the report is still not written. |
| 6 | Body does not decide competence. | Nobody becomes better or worse at their job after the flip. Only the expectations change. |
| 7 | The new expectations are social conventions, not laws of nature. | Rules appear as UI pop-ups, checklists and ads — things people made — never as biology. |
| 8 | Deadlines never change. | Running gag: "REPORT DUE: 5 PM" survives the apocalypse. |

## Tone guard-rails
- Satire targets **rules and expectations**, never people for their sex, gender identity or orientation.
- No transgender, nonbinary or intersex jokes. The magic flip is not a metaphor for, or a portrait of, transition.
- Both directions of expectation are shown as equally absurd (symmetry in every scene: Alex gets one checklist, Maya gets the opposite one).
- Numbers on screen ("+500%", "999+", "0%") are obviously fictional comedy indicators, not data.
- Ending must not claim that real inequality disappears by ignoring rules. Scene 09 carries a small line: "(real change takes more than one click)".
- Colour is never the only signal of sex or gender: every switch is also shown with shape, labels or icons.
