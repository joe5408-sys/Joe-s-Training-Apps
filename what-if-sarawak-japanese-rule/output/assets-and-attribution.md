As in episode 1, every image, map graphic, music cue and sound effect is **generated by code in this
project**. No stock footage, archival photographs, film clips, sampled music or third-party sound effects.

## 2. Voice

Same synthetic voice as episode 1 (`bm_george`, Kokoro-82M, run locally, no account, no cloning of any real
person). Pronunciation was corrected in `src/script.py`: Sarawak (sa-RAH-wak), Sarawakians, Miri (MEE-ree),
Dayak (DYE-ak), Iban (EE-bahn), Bidayuh (bi-DAH-yoo), Melanau (me-LAH-now), Rajahs.
These follow common English renderings of the local names; a Sarawakian speaker should still check them.
Recommended post text: "Narration: AI voice."

## 5. What is speculation

Everything after the imagined 1945 settlement: Japan keeping Borneo, a puppet "Borneo state", Miri oil serving
Japan for decades, Japanese-language schooling, languages surviving "mostly at home", continued resistance,
an independence movement, and the three 1970s futures. These are labelled on screen and phrased with
"might", "could" or "perhaps". The puppet-state idea is grounded in what Japan really did in Burma and the
Philippines in 1943, but it remains a guess.

## 6. Caveats

- **Illustrations are historically inspired, not reconstructions.** The bungalow is a generic Brooke-era building,
  not an exact Astana; the camp, ship and oilfield are simplified silhouettes.
- **Maps use modern borders** for orientation (e.g. Indonesia, Malaysia, and Sabah as a state name did not exist in 1941).
- **The language list is not complete.** Sarawak has many more languages (e.g. Kayan, Kenyah, Kelabit, Lun Bawang
  and other Orang Ulu languages, plus several Chinese varieties). The on-screen list ends with "ORANG ULU · AND MORE".
- **Sensitive history is summarised, not detailed.** The video does not give casualty figures or describe
  specific atrocities; anyone using it for teaching should add sources (section 4).
